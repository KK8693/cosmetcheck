# Brazil Cosmetics Compliance Guide 2025 — Complete ANVISA Handbook

Brazil is the **4th largest cosmetics market in the world** and the **1st in per-capita beauty spending**. In 2024, the sector generated **R$ 52.6 billion** (approximately $10 billion USD), with projected 8% annual growth through 2028.

But Brazil is also one of the **most regulated cosmetics markets**. The Brazilian Health Regulatory Agency (ANVISA) rejects **67% of imported cosmetic products** on first inspection. In 2024 alone, over **12,000 beauty products** were held at Brazilian customs due to compliance failures.

This guide covers everything you need to legally sell cosmetics in Brazil: product classification, ingredient restrictions, documentation requirements, costs, timelines, and common mistakes that delay approval.

---

## What is ANVISA and why it matters for cosmetics sellers

**ANVISA** (Agência Nacional de Vigilância Sanitária) is Brazil's federal health regulatory agency. For cosmetics, ANVISA controls:

- Which ingredients can be used and at what concentrations
- How products must be labeled
- Which claims are permitted vs. prohibited
- Manufacturing standards (GMP)
- Import and registration requirements

**Key fact:** Unlike the US FDA, ANVISA requires **pre-market registration** for all cosmetics. You cannot legally sell a cosmetic product in Brazil without ANVISA notification (Grau 1) or registration (Grau 2).

Penalties for non-compliance include:
- **Product seizure** at customs
- **Fines up to R$ 1.5 million** ($270,000 USD)
- **Platform delisting** on Mercado Livre, Amazon Brazil, and Shopee
- **Criminal liability** for repeat violations involving health risks

---

## ANVISA vs FDA vs EU — three completely different systems

| Aspect | ANVISA (Brazil) | FDA (USA) | EU Regulation |
|--------|----------------|-----------|---------------|
| Pre-market approval | **Required** | Not required | Notification via CPNP |
| Ingredient philosophy | **Positive list** — not approved = prohibited | Negative list — permitted unless banned | Mixed system |
| Label language | **Portuguese only** | English | Any EU language |
| Local representative | **PTR mandatory** | Not required | Responsible Person required |
| GMP requirement | **Required for registration** | Recommended | Required |
| Sunscreen regulation | **High-risk category** | OTC drug | Cosmetics |
| Timeline | **6-24 months** | Immediate | 2-6 months |

**The critical insight:** A product compliant with FDA rules may fail ANVISA inspection. US brands entering Brazil should think of ANVISA as closer to the EU regulatory model than the US model.

## Step 1: Classify your product — Grau 1 vs Grau 2

ANVISA divides cosmetics into two risk categories. Your classification determines the registration pathway, timeline, and cost.

### Grau 1 (Notificação) — Low Risk

**Products:** Basic skincare, makeup, perfume, shampoo, conditioner, soap, basic nail polish

**Requirements:**
- Online notification (free)
- Product Safety Report (PSR)
- GMP certificate
- Portuguese label
- Brazilian PTR

**Timeline:** 6-12 months

### Grau 2 (Registro) — High Risk

**Products:** Sunscreen, anti-acne treatments, anti-aging products, hair dye/bleach, baby products (under 3 years), antiperspirant, depilatory products

**Requirements:**
- Full registration application (~$400-800 fee)
- Product Safety Report
- Additional efficacy testing (SPF validation, stability, etc.)
- GMP certificate
- Portuguese label
- Brazilian PTR

**Timeline:** 12-24 months

### Quick classification test

Does your product have any of these characteristics?
- Claims SPF or sun protection → **Grau 2**
- Treats acne, wrinkles, or hair loss → **Grau 2**
- Is a hair dye, bleach, or permanent treatment → **Grau 2**
- Intended for babies or children under 3 → **Grau 2**
- Is an antiperspirant or depilatory → **Grau 2**
- None of the above → **Grau 1**

## Step 2: Check ingredients against ANVISA lists

ANVISA operates a **positive list system**. If an ingredient isn't explicitly approved, it's prohibited by default. This is stricter than the FDA and different from the EU in several areas.

### Completely banned ingredients

| Ingredient | Common Products | Why Banned |
|------------|----------------|------------|
| Hydroquinone | Skin lighteners | Carcinogenic risk |
| Mercury compounds | Whitening creams | Neurotoxicity |
| Lead and salts | Hair dyes | Developmental toxicity |
| Formaldehyde | Hair straighteners | Carcinogen |
| Corticosteroids | "Miracle" creams | Drug classification |
| Tretinoin (cosmetic) | Anti-aging | Prescription drug |

### Restricted ingredients (concentration limits)

| Ingredient | Max Concentration | Product Type |
|------------|------------------|--------------|
| Salicylic acid | 2.0% | Leave-on |
| Benzoyl peroxide | 5.0% | Anti-acne |
| PPD | 2.0% | Hair dyes |
| Phenoxyethanol | 1.0% | All products |
| MI (Methylisothiazolinone) | 0.0015% | Rinse-off only |

### UV filters — a special case

Brazil has its own approved sunscreen agent list. Several EU-approved UV filters are **not approved in Brazil**:

- **Not approved:** Tinosorb S, Tinosorb M, Uvinul A Plus
- **Approved:** Avobenzone, Octocrylene, Zinc Oxide, Titanium Dioxide

If your product claims SPF and uses a non-approved UV filter, ANVISA will reject it regardless of other compliance.

## Step 3: Prepare documentation

### Required for both Grau 1 and Grau 2:

1. **Complete INCI ingredient list** with exact concentrations
2. **Product Safety Report (PSR)** — prepared by your PTR
3. **GMP certificate** — ISO 22716 accepted, must be current
4. **Portuguese label** — all text in Portuguese per RDC 752/2022
5. **Brazilian PTR designation** — licensed pharmacist or chemist

### Additional for Grau 2:

6. **Efficacy test reports** — SPF validation for sunscreens
7. **Stability testing** — 3-month accelerated minimum
8. **Additional toxicological data** — for active ingredients

### Label requirements (RDC 752/2022)

Your Portuguese label must include:
- Product name
- CNPJ of registration holder
- PTR name
- Complete ingredient list (descending order)
- Mode of use and precautions
- Expiration date and batch number
- ANVISA registration/notification number
- Net content
- Country of origin

**Common rejection:** Labels that are "mostly Portuguese" with some English text. ANVISA requires **100% Portuguese**.

## Step 4: Appoint a Brazilian PTR

The **Responsável Técnico (PTR)** is a licensed professional (pharmacist or chemist) who serves as your product's legal representative in Brazil. Their name appears on the product label.

### Options:

| Approach | Cost | Best For |
|----------|------|----------|
| Hire regulatory consultant as PTR | $2,000-5,000/year | Small brands, 1-10 SKUs |
| Partner with Brazilian distributor | Negotiated margin | Brands seeking distribution |
| Establish Brazilian subsidiary | $10,000+ | Large brands with Brazil focus |

**Important:** Your PTR must be experienced with ANVISA cosmetics regulation. A general pharmacist without ANVISA experience will slow down your application.

## Step 5: Submit and track registration

### Grau 1 (Notification)

1. Log into ANVISA's Sistema de Notificação
2. Complete product information form
3. Upload PSR and GMP documentation
4. Submit — no government fee
5. Wait for technical review (6-12 months)

### Grau 2 (Registration)

1. Log into ANVISA's Sistema de Peticionamento
2. Complete detailed application form
3. Upload all documentation + test reports
4. Pay government fee (~$400-800)
5. Wait for technical review (12-24 months)

### Tracking and complementation

ANVISA may request "complementation" — additional documents or corrections. Each round adds 2-4 months. Common complementation requests:

- Ingredient list doesn't match PSR
- Label doesn't comply with RDC 752/2022
- GMP certificate expired or improperly notarized
- Missing PTR designation
- Product claims trigger drug classification

**Strategy:** Submit your hero SKU first. Use feedback to improve subsequent applications rather than submitting all SKUs simultaneously.

## Common mistakes and how to avoid them

### Mistake 1: Assuming FDA/EU compliance = ANVISA compliance

**Reality:** ANVISA has unique restrictions. Several EU-approved ingredients are not approved in Brazil.

**Solution:** Always check your ingredient list against ANVISA's specific positive/negative lists.

### Mistake 2: Using English labels

**Reality:** ANVISA requires 100% Portuguese labeling. Even "Net Wt." or "Made in" must be translated.

**Solution:** Use a regulatory translator, not a general translator. Regulatory terminology matters.

### Mistake 3: Incomplete ingredient disclosure

**Reality:** "Fragrance" isn't sufficient. ANVISA requires disclosure of fragrance components that are restricted allergens.

**Solution:** Request the full formula from your manufacturer, not just the retail label.

### Mistake 4: Drug claims on cosmetic products

**Reality:** Words like "treats," "cures," "heals," "anti-inflammatory" trigger drug classification.

**Solution:** Use cosmetic terminology: "helps with," "reduces appearance of," "supports."

### Mistake 5: Submitting all SKUs at once

**Reality:** If your first application has errors, all simultaneous applications will have the same errors.

**Solution:** Submit one SKU, wait for feedback, then submit the rest with corrections.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I sell cosmetics online in Brazil without ANVISA registration?

**No.** ANVISA requires registration for ALL cosmetics sold in Brazil, regardless of channel. This includes e-commerce, social media sales, and dropshipping. There is no "small quantity" or "personal use" exemption for commercial sellers.

### How much does ANVISA registration cost per product?

**Grau 1:** $5,000-10,000 per SKU (including PTR, documentation, consultant)
**Grau 2:** $10,000-25,000 per SKU (including testing)

### How long does ANVISA registration take?

**Grau 1:** 6-12 months
**Grau 2:** 12-24 months
Sunscreens may take 12-18 months even for Grau 2.

### Can I use the same product label in Brazil and Mexico?

**No.** Brazil requires Portuguese; Mexico requires Spanish. The ingredient nomenclature also differs. You need separate labels for each market.

### Do I need a Brazilian company to sell cosmetics in Brazil?

**No.** You can sell through a Brazilian distributor who holds the registration, or hire a PTR as your local representative. However, establishing a local entity may be beneficial for large-scale operations.

### What happens if my product is rejected?

You'll receive a detailed rejection notice specifying the issues. You can correct and resubmit, but each resubmission restarts the review timeline. Most rejections are due to documentation errors, not product safety issues.
